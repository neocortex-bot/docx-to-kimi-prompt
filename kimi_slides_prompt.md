## SECTION 3: DISCUSSION (13-15 slides)

### Sub-section 3.1 — Basic Disease: Conduction Disorders

### Slide 6 — Classification of Bradyarrhythmias (ESC 2021)
- Definition: Disorders of impulse formation or conduction → low heart rate
- Classification (ESC 2021, Glikson et al.):
  * Intrinsic: structural/functional abnormalities of conduction system
  * Extrinsic: autonomic, drugs, metabolic, reversible causes
- SND spectrum: sinus bradycardia, sinus arrest/pause, SA block, brady-tachy syndrome
- AV block grades: first-degree (PR >0.2s), second-degree (Mobitz I/II), third-degree
- Key point: SND + first-degree AVB = multilevel conduction disease
- Visual: Gambar 3.1 (bradyarrhythmia algorithm from ESC 2021)

### Slide 7 — Sinus Pause, Junctional Escape & First-Degree AVB
- Definition sinus pause: absent P wave for several seconds
- Junctional escape: backup rhythm from AV junction, QRS narrow (<0.12s)
- First-degree AVB: PR >0.2s; Class IIa for pacing if PR >0.3s WITH symptoms (ESC 2021)
- Application to case (3 bullet points):
  * Sinus pause + junctional escape (QRS 0.04s) → SND with adequate escape mechanism
  * PR 0.36s >0.3s threshold + syncope + weakness → meets IIa indication
  * Multilevel involvement: SAN (pause) + AVN (PR prolongation)

### Slide 8 — Diabetes & the Conduction System
- Three mechanisms (diagram concept):
  1. AGEs → myocardial fibrosis → conduction disruption (Aronson, 2002)
  2. CAN → autonomic dysregulation → HR variability loss (Serhiyenko & Serhiyenko, 2018)
  3. Microvascular ischemia → nodal dysfunction
- Prevalence data:
  * First-degree AVB in DM: 5.7% vs non-DM (Shrivastava et al., 2025)
  * CAN prevalence in DM: up to 60-90% (Osailan et al., 2021)
  * CAN with retinopathy: 89.28% (Paneerselvam et al., 2019)
- Application to case: DM 15 yrs + HbA1c 9.6% + retinopathy → very high CAN probability

### Slide 9 — Reversible vs Intrinsic Causes
- ESC 2021: Exclude reversible causes before PPM (drugs, MI, toxins, infection, surgery, electrolytes)
- Our patient's reversibility table:
  Factor | Present | After correction | Effect on conduction
  Hyponatremia (125) | Yes | Corrected | PR still prolonged
  CAP (CRP 194.6) | Yes | Antibiotics | PR still prolonged
  AKI (eGFR 16) | Yes | Nephrology Rx | PR improved but still abnormal
- Conclusion: Conduction disturbance is INTRINSIC and PERSISTENT
- Syncope history 2 years supports chronicity

### Sub-section 3.2 — Comprehensive Analysis: Hemodynamics

### Slide 10 — Oxygen Delivery (DO₂) in Low CO States
- CaO₂ formula: (1.34 × Hb × SaO₂) + (0.0031 × PaO₂) (Saugel et al., 2013)
- DO₂ = CO × CaO₂ × 10
- Calculation for our patient:
  CaO₂ = (1.34 × 9.7 × 0.995) + (0.0031 × 166.6) = 13.46 mL O₂/dL
  DO₂ = 3.3 × 13.46 × 10 = 444 mL/min (normal 934 ± 288)
  DO₂ index = 295 mL/min/m² (normal 520-720)
- Key insight: Low DO₂ is driven by LOW CO, not poor oxygenation
- SaO₂ 99.5% and PaO₂ 166.6 confirm lungs are working normally

### Slide 11 — Compensatory Vasoconstriction
- Physiology cascade (flow diagram):
  Low CO → Baroreceptor activation → Sympathetic ↑ → Peripheral vasoconstriction → SVR ↑
- Our patient: SVR 2,109 dyn·s·cm⁻⁵ (normal 800-1,200)
- Effect on perfusion:
  * Blood shunted to vital organs (brain, heart, kidneys)
  * Peripheral perfusion ↓ ↓ ↓
  * PPG signal amplitude ↓
  * Pulse oximetry becomes inaccurate

### Sub-section 3.3 — Core Topic: SpO₂-SaO₂ Discrepancy

### Slide 12 — How Pulse Oximetry Works
- Principle: Red (660nm) + Infrared (940nm) light absorption by hemoglobin
- Requires PULSATILE signal (PPG) = AC/DC components
- Perfusion Index (PI) = AC/DC ratio — reflects peripheral perfusion
- Low PI → weak signal → inaccurate SpO₂ reading
- Visual: PPG waveform diagram (AC + DC components)
- Key message: "Pulse oximetry measures pulsatile blood flow, not just oxygenation"

### Slide 13 — The Discrepancy: Our Case
- Striking comparison (large text/visual):
  SpO₂ 81% | SaO₂ 99.5% | PaO₂ 166.6 mmHg
- Gap: 18.5 percentage points between SpO₂ and SaO₂
- Evidence for mechanism (3 bullets):
  1. Post-TPM: SpO₂ ↑ without FiO₂ change → confirms perfusion, not ventilation
  2. Day 2 (AF, CO 5.9): SpO₂ 98% matching SaO₂ 99.9%
  3. Temporal correlation: CO ↑ → SVR ↓ → SpO₂ ↑
- Conclusion: Vasoconstriction from low CO → PPG ↓ → SpO₂ falsely low

### Slide 14 — Evidence from Literature (Low Perfusion Accuracy)
- Giuliano et al. (2023) — J Clin Monit Comput:
  * Pulse ox accuracy decreases in low perfusion states
  * PI classified as low, very low, ultra-low
  * Significant inter-device variability
- Karlsson et al. (2026) — J Intensive Care:
  * Before thermal intervention: bias +4.09% (LoA 0.61-7.56%)
  * After intervention PI ↑ 0.56→3.59: bias 0.00% (LoA -1.22 to 1.23)
  * PI <0.3%: readings unreliable
- Jung et al. (2026) — Scientific Reports:
  * PPG amplitude ↓ 41% during vasoconstriction
  * IPG less affected than PPG

### Slide 15 — Differential Diagnosis of SpO₂-SaO₂ Discrepancy
- Table format:
  Condition | SpO₂ | SaO₂ | PaO₂ | Key Differentiator
  Low perfusion (our case) | ↓↓ | Normal | Normal | ↑ SpO₂ post CO ↑
  COHbemia | ~95% | ~97% | Normal | COHb >10% on co-oximetry
  MetHbemia | ~85% | ~92% | Normal | MetHb >15%
  Dye interference | ↓ | Normal | Normal | Recent dye exposure
  Lab error | Normal | ↓ | Normal | Repeat ABG normal
- Our case: All arrows point to LOW PERFUSION as cause

### Slide 16 — Role of Diabetic Autonomic Neuropathy (CAN)
- CAN affects:
  * Heart rate variability ↓ (Ewing's score: HR response to Valsalva, deep breathing, standing)
  * Baroreflex dysfunction ↓
  * Vascular tone regulation ↓
- Our patient: DM 15 yrs + HbA1c 9.6% + retinopathy → very high CAN probability
- CAN may blunt appropriate autonomic responses to low CO
- However, vasoconstriction still occurred (SVR 2,109) — sympathetic efferent pathway intact
- References: Serhiyenko & Serhiyenko (2018), Osailan et al. (2021), Paneerselvam et al. (2019)

## SECTION 4: SUMMARY & CONCLUSION (2 slides)

### Slide 23 — Key Takeaways (4 points)
- 1. Low SpO₂ ≠ hypoxemia — Always verify with ABG when pulse oximetry doesn't match the clinical picture
- 2. Mechanism: Low CO → compensatory vasoconstriction (SVR ↑) → ↓ PPG amplitude → inaccurate SpO₂ reading
- 3. TPM as diagnostic tool: SpO₂ improvement post-pacing without FiO₂ change confirms hemodynamic mechanism
- 4. PPM indicated: Symptomatic SND + PR >0.3s + brady-tachy syndrome → intrinsic, non-reversible
- Bottom line (bold): "When the pulse oximeter lies, look at the heart, not the lungs."
